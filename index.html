Methods

Animal cohorts and sampled tissues

To examine N-glycosylation across organs and ages, we analyzed several mouse cohorts. Brain, spleen, liver, kidney, and plasma came from 54 male C57BL/6JN mice sampled at nine ages from 1 to 28 months (six per age). Purified myelin came from separate cohorts: 18 males at 3, 16, and 29 months (six per age) and 12 females at 3, 13, and 27 months (five, three, and four mice, respectively). These are the datasets you can currently explore in this atlas.

Glycoproteomics and proteomics workflows

To assess N-glycosylation at individual glycosites, we first isolated proteins from each sample. We used a high-throughput magnetic bead strategy to capture proteins and digested them with trypsin. We reserved a fraction of the resulting peptides for tandem mass tag (TMT) proteomics. From the rest, we enriched glycopeptides using beads functionalized with phenylboronic acid and analyzed them by LC–MS/MS. We searched the spectra with MSFragger and applied additional quality control: we retained glycan assignments with q < 0.05, unique peptide backbones, noncontaminant proteins, and an unambiguous match to one glycosite on one protein.

Glycosite coverage tiers

Although all glycoPSMs pass quality filters, coverage can vary widely between glycosites. Some glycosites from highly abundant proteins can be sampled hundreds of times per replicate, while other glycosites may only be observed a handful of times. We therefore developed a tiering system to classify coverage of glycosites. Some tiers are only suitable for descriptions of glycosite composition while others provide sufficient information to make significance calls about inter-organ differences and age-related changes.

Reducing glycan complexity to glycan types

Across all 270 atlas samples, 931 unique N-glycans were identified, showcasing the profound heterogeneity of N-glycosylation. This heterogeneity can make calculating compositional changes challenging. We therefore classified these glycans into 8 overall glycan types based on their size and monosaccharide composition. The eight glycan types comprise stubs, paucimannose, high mannose, phospho, undecorated, fucosylated, sialylated, and jointly fucosylated & sialylated glycans.

Glycosite heterogeneity calculations

Two sites can have the same number of observed glycan types but very different distributions. To quantify this compositional distribution, we calculate the percent abundance of each glycan type at a given glycosite. This calculation is based on the number of glycoPSMs for a given glycan type divided by the total number of glycoPSMs identified at that glycosite. The composition at a glycosite can therefore be expressed as a vector of percentages (e.g. 70%, 10%, 10%, 10%). Shannon entropy (H) measures how evenly the glycoPSMs are divided among those glycan types. We express the result as weighted glycan types, exp(H). This value is 1 if every glycoPSM belongs to one glycan type and equals the observed glycan type count if all glycan types are equally abundant. In our example here, the 70%, 10%, 10%, 10% distribution has H = 0.94 and 2.56 weighted glycan types; the even 25% mix has H = 1.39 and 4.00 weighted glycan types.

Quantifying differences in glycosite composition

Total variation distance (TVD) compares the proportions of the eight glycan types in two compositions. It is 0% when the compositions are identical and 100% when they have no types in common. The example sites have a TVD of 45%, so we would classify them as partially remodeled.

Site level composition and summary metrics

The collection of all these metrics allows us to describe any glycosite in our dataset and compare it to any other glycosite. This same principle allows us to compare any two sets of compositions. This could be the same glycosite across organs or different ages within the same organ.

Testing age-related glycosite composition

Glycan composition varies from animal to animal, even at the same glycosite. This reflects both variation between animals and the inherent variability in glycoPSM sampling and glycosite coverage. We therefore want to find changes associated with age that are robust. To test whether a glycosite changes with age, we calculate glycan type percent abundance at each glycosite for each animal. We then average the measured animals in the young and old groups, giving each animal equal weight. The top bars show each animal’s composition; the middle bars show the group means. Total variation distance (TVD) summarizes the difference between those means, from 0% for identical compositions to 100% when they share no glycan types.

To assess whether the observed TVD stands out from variation among animals, we shuffle the young and old labels while keeping the group sizes fixed. We recalculate the group means and TVD after each shuffle. The bottom histogram shows the TVDs produced by these shuffles, and the blue line marks the observed TVD. If k of B shuffles produce a TVD at least as large as the observed one, p = (k + 1)/(B + 1). The p value’s resolution is therefore limited by the shuffles performed and the number of label assignments the replicates permit. With 18 animals in each group, there are 36 choose 18 (approximately 9.1 billion) possible assignments; with 99,999,999 shuffles, the smallest reportable p value is 10⁻⁸.

Lamc1 N574 shows a consistent shift toward undecorated glycans with age (TVD 18.61%, p = 7 × 10⁻⁸).

Nfasc N1047 has different young and old group means (TVD 12.77%), but this observed difference falls within the range produced by shuffled labels and is therefore not significant (p = 0.125).

Thy1 N94 has nearly identical group means and shuffling does not change that (TVD 0.56%, p = 0.675).

Reading the atlas

Methods summary

This summary explains the calculations used in the browser. Full experimental methods will accompany the manuscript.

Cohorts and measurements

The five-organ atlas samples male C57BL/6JN mice at nine ages from 1 to 28 months, with six animals per age. TMT proteomics measures protein abundance; enriched glycopeptide LC-MS/MS measures N-glycan composition. Male and female myelin are separate cohorts with their own sampled ages.

Within-site composition and TVD

Glycan-type percentages use eight composition-based classes. Pooled profiles divide each class’s glyco-PSMs by all glyco-PSMs at that site. TVD (%) = ½ × Σ|Aᵢ − Bᵢ| when A and B are percentages. Identical profiles have TVD 0%; non-overlapping profiles have TVD 100%. Signed class differences are reported in percentage points.

Age comparisons

The browser preserves TVD and raw permutation p-values from the versioned tested-site exports. Atlas age comparisons require Tier 2+ coverage, at least nine detected animals, and at least 18 glyco-PSMs in each compared bin. Myelin uses cohort-specific eligibility in its source exports. Age gains and losses use differences of per-animal mean percentages. A type is dominant when it accounts for at least 60% of total gain or total loss; all other directions are mixed. Hits use raw p <0.05 and are exploratory.

Inter-organ comparisons and heterogeneity

Site profiles pool glyco-PSMs across all detected ages and samples in each dataset. Comparisons require at least five PSMs at the shared site in both datasets (Tier 1+ in atlas organs). Conserved: TVD <20%; partially shifted: 20% to <50%; strongly remodeled: ≥50%. Young-only reference comparisons pool young-bin PSMs and require Tier 2+, nine detected animals, and 18 PSMs in each young bin.

Shannon H = −Σpᵢ ln(pᵢ), with p expressed as proportions. Effective glycan types = exp(H). Low diversity is <1.5 effective types, intermediate is 1.5 to <2.5, and high is ≥2.5. Box plots show every site, medians and interquartile ranges; whiskers extend to the most extreme point within 1.5 times the interquartile range.
